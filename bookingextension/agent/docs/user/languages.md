# Working in your own language

The Booking Wizard speaks your language. You write in whatever language you like, and it replies in that language. You don't set anything up first — just type, and it follows your lead.

## Write in any language

Send your request in the language that's natural for you. The wizard understands free text in many languages and works out what you want. For example, all of these do the same thing *(the example requires mod_booking)*:

- **English:** "Create a booking option called Morning Yoga on Mondays at 7am."
- **German:** "Erstelle eine Buchungsoption namens Morgen-Yoga, montags um 7 Uhr."
- **French:** "Crée une option de réservation appelée Yoga du matin, le lundi à 7h."
- **Spanish:** "Crea una opción de reserva llamada Yoga matutino, los lunes a las 7."
- **Italian:** "Crea un'opzione di prenotazione chiamata Yoga del mattino, il lunedì alle 7."

You'll get the reply — the answer, the explanation of the preview, the confirmation question, and the result — in the language you wrote in.

## It replies in your language

The wizard matches the language of your message. If you write in Portuguese, its previews and answers come back in Portuguese. If you ask a question in Polish, you get a Polish answer. This includes:

- The **preview** of any change it's about to make.
- The **question** asking you to confirm.
- Its **answers** to your read-only questions.
- Any **clarification** it asks for when it needs you to pick an item or fill in a detail.

The chat panel itself — buttons such as *Confirm*, headings, and the labels in a preview card — follows your **Moodle interface language**, like the rest of Moodle. The wizard ships with English and German interface texts.

So the whole conversation stays in one language unless you change it.

## Switch languages whenever you like

You're not locked into one language for the session. You can switch between messages and the wizard keeps up. For instance:

> **You:** Show me the booking options in this activity.
>
> **Wizard:** *(answers in English)*
>
> **You:** Erstelle eine neue Option namens Abendkurs am Dienstag um 18 Uhr.
>
> **Wizard:** *(replies in German, previews the change, asks to confirm)*

A **new request** in another language switches the conversation to that language. A short **answer** to a question or confirmation of the wizard — "yes", "sí", "the second one" — keeps the current language. This is handy if you usually work in one language but want to phrase one request in another.

## A few practical notes

- **Names stay as you write them.** If you name a booking option, a place, or a person, the wizard uses exactly the text you typed — it won't translate the names of your items.
- **Dates and numbers** are understood in common local forms, but if anything is ambiguous, the wizard will ask, in your language, to be sure.
- **The content you create** is in whatever language you wrote it. If you describe a booking option in French, its title and description are saved in French.
- **Confirmation still applies.** Switching language doesn't change how safety works — any change is still previewed and waits for your OK. See [confirmations and safety](confirmations-and-safety.md).

## Example: a full exchange in two languages

> **You:** ¿Qué hay en este curso?
>
> **Wizard:** *(lists the sections and activities, in Spanish)*
>
> **You:** Now add a page called Field Trip to the General section.
>
> **Wizard:** I'll add the page *Field Trip* to the General section. Confirm? *(in English — a new request switches the language)*
>
> **You:** Sí, adelante.
>
> **Wizard:** Done — the page *Field Trip* has been added. *(still in English — "Sí" answers the confirmation)*

The wizard follows each new request and keeps the language while you answer its questions. Write however you're most comfortable — it'll meet you there.
