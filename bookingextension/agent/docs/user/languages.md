# Working in your own language

The Booking Assistant speaks your language. You write in whatever language you like, and it replies in the same one. You don't set anything up first — just type, and it follows your lead.

## Write in any language

Send your request in the language that's natural for you. The assistant understands free text in many languages and works out what you want. For example, all of these do the same thing:

- **English:** "Create a booking option called Morning Yoga on Mondays at 7am."
- **German:** "Erstelle eine Buchungsoption namens Morgen-Yoga, montags um 7 Uhr."
- **French:** "Crée une option de réservation appelée Yoga du matin, le lundi à 7h."
- **Spanish:** "Crea una opción de reserva llamada Yoga matutino, los lunes a las 7."
- **Italian:** "Crea un'opzione di prenotazione chiamata Yoga del mattino, il lunedì alle 7."

You'll get the reply — the preview, the confirmation question, and the result — in the same language you wrote in.

## It replies in your language

The assistant matches the language of your message. If you write in Portuguese, its previews and answers come back in Portuguese. If you ask a question in Polish, you get a Polish answer. This includes:

- The **preview** of any change it's about to make.
- The **question** asking you to confirm.
- Its **answers** to your read-only questions.
- Any **clarification** it asks for when it needs you to pick an item or fill in a detail.

So the whole conversation stays in one language unless you change it.

## Switch languages whenever you like

You're not locked into one language for the session. You can switch between messages and the assistant keeps up. For instance:

> **You:** Show me the booking options in this activity.
>
> **Assistant:** *(answers in English)*
>
> **You:** Erstelle eine neue Option namens Abendkurs am Dienstag um 18 Uhr.
>
> **Assistant:** *(replies in German, previews the change, asks to confirm)*

Each message is answered in the language you wrote it in. This is handy if you usually work in one language but want to phrase one request in another, or if colleagues share the same conversation.

## A few practical notes

- **Names stay as you write them.** If you name a booking option, a place, or a person, the assistant uses exactly the text you typed — it won't translate the names of your items.
- **Dates and numbers** are understood in common local forms, but if anything is ambiguous, the assistant will ask, in your language, to be sure.
- **The content you create** is in whatever language you wrote it. If you describe a booking option in French, its title and description are saved in French.
- **Confirmation still applies.** Switching language doesn't change how safety works — any change is still previewed and waits for your OK. See [confirmations and safety](confirmations-and-safety.md).

## Example: a full exchange in two languages

> **You:** ¿Quién está inscrito en este curso?
>
> **Assistant:** *(lists the enrolled users, in Spanish)*
>
> **You:** Now create a booking option named Field Trip on 10 May at 9am.
>
> **Assistant:** I'll create *Field Trip*, 10 May 09:00, in this activity. Confirm? *(in English)*
>
> **You:** Sí, adelante.
>
> **Assistant:** Listo, he creado *Field Trip*. *(in Spanish)*

The assistant simply follows each message. Write however you're most comfortable — it'll meet you there.
