# Choosing the right item

When you ask the Booking Assistant to do something, it has to work out *which* thing you mean — which booking option, which activity, which course, which user. This page explains how it targets the right one, and how you can steer it.

## Name things specifically

The clearer you are about what you mean, the more directly the assistant lands on it. Use names, not just "it" or "that one", especially at the start of a request:

- Instead of "edit the option", say "edit the **Beginner Yoga** option".
- Instead of "this course", name it: "in the **Spring Pottery** course".
- Instead of "that user", give a name: "show **Maria Sanchez**'s enrolment status".

Specific names — of booking options, places, trainers, courses, or people — help the assistant target exactly what you intend, with fewer back-and-forth questions.

## It works in the context you're in

The assistant pays attention to where you are. If you opened the chat inside a **course** or a **Booking activity**, it assumes you mean that one unless you tell it otherwise. So when you're already inside the Friday Fitness Booking activity and you say "list the options" or "create a new option", it works on that activity — you don't have to name it every time.

This makes everyday work quick: open the activity you're managing, and just talk about "this option", "here", "this course". The assistant knows what "here" means.

## If several things match, it asks you to pick

The assistant doesn't guess when it's unsure. If your request could match more than one item — say there are three options with "Yoga" in the name, or two users called "Tom" — it stops and asks you to choose, rather than picking one and risking the wrong target.

> **You:** Change the price of the Yoga class to 20.
>
> **Assistant:** I found a few options with "Yoga": *Beginner Yoga*, *Morning Yoga*, *Yoga Flow*. Which one do you mean?
>
> **You:** Morning Yoga.
>
> **Assistant:** I'll set the price of *Morning Yoga* to 20.00. Confirm?

You just reply with the one you want — by name, or "the second one" — and it continues. This is also why nothing is changed until you confirm: the assistant makes sure it's aimed at the right item first. See [confirmations and safety](confirmations-and-safety.md).

## You can point it at another activity or course

Even when you're inside one activity, you can tell the assistant to target a different one. Just name it in your request:

- "Create this option in the **Evening Classes** Booking activity instead."
- "Show me the options in the **Summer Camp** course."
- "Add a forum to the **Photography 101** course."

Naming another course or activity overrides the current context for that request. If the name you give matches several places, the assistant asks you to pick, the same as above.

## Give the details it needs

Sometimes the assistant has the right item but is missing a detail it needs to act — a date, a price, which course. When that happens, it asks:

> **You:** Create a booking option called Night Hike.
>
> **Assistant:** Sure — what date and time should Night Hike be, and in which activity?

Answer the question and it carries on. You can also front-load the details to skip the question: "Create a booking option called Night Hike on 12 April at 20:00 in this activity."

## Tips for getting it right first time

- **Lead with the name** of the option, course, or person.
- **Say "this" or "here"** when you mean the activity or course you've opened — that's exactly what it expects.
- **Name a different target explicitly** when you want to act somewhere else.
- **Include the key details** (date, time, place, price, trainer) up front for changes, so there's less back-and-forth.
- **When it asks you to pick, just pick** — by name or position. It won't proceed until it's sure which item you mean.
