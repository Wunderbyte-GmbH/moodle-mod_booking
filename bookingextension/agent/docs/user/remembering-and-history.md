# Remembering preferences and recalling earlier conversations

The assistant can remember things you tell it to remember — your preferences and defaults — so you don't have to repeat them every time. It can also recall what you worked on in earlier conversations. You stay in control: you can list what it remembers and tell it to forget anything.

Everything the assistant remembers is **tied to your own account**. Your preferences and history are yours; they aren't shared with other users, and you only ever see your own.

## Asking it to remember a preference

Tell the assistant a preference in plain language, and it keeps it for next time. This is handy for defaults you use again and again — a typical number of places, a default language, a way you like things named.

Example requests:

- "Always default to 20 places when I create a booking option."
- "Remember that I usually run sessions in the afternoon."
- "From now on, set the default language to German for my options."
- "Remember that my courses use a 24-hour cancellation deadline."
- "Please keep in mind that I prefer short confirmations."

The assistant confirms what it's noted, and applies it in later requests where it fits.

> **You:** Always default to 20 places when I create a booking option.
>
> **Assistant:** Noted — I'll use 20 places as the default when you create a booking option, unless you say otherwise. You can change or remove this any time.

A remembered preference is a starting point, not a cage: you can override it in any single request ("this one's 30 places"), and that doesn't change what you've asked it to remember.

## Listing what it remembers

You can ask the assistant what it currently remembers about your preferences, so you can check what's in there.

Example requests:

- "What do you remember about me?"
- "List my saved preferences."
- "What defaults are you using for me?"
- "Show me everything you're keeping in mind."

> **You:** What do you remember about me?
>
> **Assistant:** Here's what I'm keeping in mind for you:
> - Default to **20 places** on new booking options.
> - Prefer **German** as the default language.
> - Use a **24-hour cancellation deadline**.

## Forgetting something

If a preference is out of date or you'd rather it didn't apply, ask the assistant to forget it. You can drop a single preference or clear them all.

Example requests:

- "Forget that I default to 20 places."
- "Stop defaulting to German."
- "Remove the 24-hour cancellation preference."
- "Forget everything you remember about me."

> **You:** Forget that I default to 20 places.
>
> **Assistant:** Done — I've removed the 20-places default. I'll ask for the number of places when you create an option, unless you tell me a new default.

## Recalling an earlier conversation

The assistant can look back over what you did together earlier and remind you. This is useful when you pick up where you left off — "what did we set up yesterday?" — and you don't want to scroll back through everything.

Example requests:

- "What did we set up yesterday?"
- "Remind me what we changed on the First Aid course last week."
- "What options did I create with you earlier?"
- "Did we sort out the Tuesday yoga session in our last conversation?"
- "Summarise what we worked on this morning."

> **You:** What did we set up yesterday?
>
> **Assistant:** Yesterday we worked on the **Spring Wellness** course. You created the **Tuesday Yoga** option with 12 places, set a 24-hour cancellation deadline, and added Maria Huber and Tom Berger to it.

Because your history is masked in the saved record for privacy, the assistant recalls the gist of what you did rather than exposing personal details in the clear. You still see the names and details you're entitled to see during your conversation. See [privacy](privacy.md) for how this works.

## It's per your account

What the assistant remembers — both your stated preferences and your conversation history — belongs to **your account only**. Another user signing in won't see your preferences or your past conversations, and you won't see theirs. If you share a course with a colleague, your remembered defaults still don't carry over to them.
